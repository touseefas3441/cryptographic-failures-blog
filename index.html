1. Why do cryptographic failures matter?

Most websites deal with information that should not be seen by everyone. This could be a password, payment information, personal details or a private message.

Cryptography is used to help protect this information. It can be used to encrypt data, protect passwords and secure communication between a user and a website.

The problem is that cryptography can also be used incorrectly. A website might use an old algorithm, store passwords in an unsafe way, or accidentally expose a secret key.

These types of problems are known as cryptographic failures.

In the current OWASP Top 10:2025, Cryptographic Failures is listed as A04:2025. OWASP includes issues such as weak cryptography, exposed keys, poor password storage and problems with random number generation in this category.

2. Who is affected?

The most obvious group is the users of a website. If sensitive information is not properly protected, their accounts or personal information could be exposed.

Developers are also involved because many cryptographic failures are caused by the way security is implemented or configured.

Organizations can also be affected. A security incident involving sensitive information can create financial, legal and reputational problems.

3. What will you learn?

In this blog, I will explain what cryptographic failures are and show some common ways they can happen.

I will also look at two real-world cases and discuss what can be learned from them.

The blog then covers some ways developers can reduce these risks. There is also a small browser-based demonstration showing how a cryptographic hash changes when the input changes.

The demonstration is intentionally simple and does not test or attack any real system.
