What is the Risk?

Cryptographic failures occur when sensitive information is not properly protected by encryption, hashing, secure random numbers, or appropriate key management.

The main risk is that information intended to remain confidential or protected may become accessible to unauthorized people.

Simple idea: Weak or missing cryptographic protection can turn private information into exposed information.

1. Important Assets at Risk

Cryptographic failures can affect many types of information and security assets.

  • User passwords
  • Personal information
  • Financial and payment information
  • Authentication tokens and session information
  • Private messages and documents
  • Encryption keys and secrets
  • Business and organizational information

2. Possible Threat Actors

Different types of attackers may try to take advantage of cryptographic weaknesses.

  • Cybercriminals: may attempt to obtain valuable information for financial gain.
  • Opportunistic attackers: may search for systems that expose sensitive information because of weak security controls.
  • Malicious insiders: authorized users may misuse information if security controls are weak.
  • Other unauthorized users: people who gain access to exposed data may attempt to use it for further attacks.

3. Common Causes

Cryptographic failures can happen for several reasons.

  • Using outdated or inappropriate cryptographic algorithms
  • Sending sensitive information without adequate encryption
  • Storing passwords incorrectly
  • Using weak or predictable random numbers
  • Poor management of encryption keys
  • Incorrect TLS configuration
  • Hard-coded cryptographic secrets
  • Using encryption without properly protecting the encryption keys

4. Potential Impact

Impact Area Possible Consequence
Confidentiality Sensitive information may be exposed to unauthorized people.
Privacy Personal information may be disclosed.
Financial Security Exposed financial information may contribute to fraud or financial loss.
Account Security Weak password protection may make unauthorized account access easier.
Reputation An organization may lose customer trust after a security incident.
Legal and Regulatory Organizations may face obligations or consequences related to protecting personal or regulated information.

5. Risk Chain

A cryptographic failure can be understood as a chain of events:

Cryptographic weakness → Information becomes insufficiently protected → Unauthorized access or disclosure → Potential security, privacy or financial impact

6. Ethical Considerations

Studying cryptographic failures is important for improving security, but security testing must be performed responsibly.

A security researcher or student should not test cryptographic weaknesses against systems without permission. Demonstrations should use intentionally created examples, local environments, fake information, or purpose-built security laboratories.

This project therefore uses safe educational demonstrations rather than testing real organizations or real users.

Key Lesson

Cryptography is not only about choosing an encryption algorithm. Secure systems also require appropriate algorithms, secure implementation, strong key management, safe password storage, secure random values, and correct configuration.
